# 3V3 half-court

- The ballhandler uses the 1V1 shot timing, archetype shot selection, dribble moves, contact and finish logic. The 3V3 controller adds directional passing (E / gamepad B / touch 传球), a call for the ball from CPU teammates, and short passes that defenders can intercept.
- Like 5V5’s `r5Of`, the controller reads star-specific ratings from `r5.js` with fallback ratings for stars outside that table. The 5V5 `handlerInp0` / `passVal` ideas are simplified here: pass IQ and vision inform the decision to pass, with an emphasis on open cutters and drive-and-kick passes. Pass accuracy affects delivery speed; pass perception affects interceptions.
- The `offBall` and `offBallD` ideas are modeled as three-out spacing, arc spots for shooters, timed cuts for strong finishers, and man assignments that shade toward the ball on drives. Cut timing depends on offensive IQ; help depends on help-defense IQ and how dangerous it would be to leave the assigned shooter. No scripted screens, full-court transitions, or 5V5 shot bonuses.
- After a make the other team checks the ball outside the arc at the top; that check satisfies the clearance requirement. Live-ball changes inside the arc require clearing before a shot can count. 3V3 ends at the selected target, without a two-point lead requirement.
- For online play, select the same mode in both browsers. One player creates a room and shares the code; the other joins and waits for the host to start. In 3V3, joins take open waiting slots, and any empty ones are filled by CPUs on start. The host simulates the match and guests send inputs over PeerJS/WebRTC; connectivity depends on the public signaling service and the players’ networks.

Run `node qa/3v3.cjs` for headless possession, scoring, clear-arc, passing, shot-clock and full CPU-match checks.
